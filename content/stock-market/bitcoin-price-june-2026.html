Why Is Bitcoin Falling in June 2026?

Five factors are driving the current selloff simultaneously — a rare confluence that has pushed Bitcoin below levels not seen since before the Iran conflict began.

1. US–Iran War Uncertainty

Bitcoin had been climbing since the war started, peaking above $82,000 by mid-May. But as the conflict dragged on with no resolution in sight — including Hezbollah rejecting Israel's ceasefire offer — investors began treating crypto as a risk asset rather than a safe haven. Bitcoin opened the war at $65,878 and erased every dollar of gains made since.

2. Sticky Inflation and Fed Rate Uncertainty

Traders reacted to sticky inflation concerns, uncertainty around Federal Reserve rate cuts, and renewed US dollar strength. When rates stay high, investors often prefer cash, bonds, or gold instead of volatile assets. A stronger dollar also makes Bitcoin less attractive for global buyers.

3. Spot Bitcoin ETF Outflows

Spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $1.42 billion in outflows as investor sentiment turned risk-off. Investors are moving out of crypto amid continued market uncertainty, with higher-performing sectors such as AI offering alternatives.

4. Technical Breakdown at $73,000

BTC lost support near $73,000 and moved closer to the $70,000 zone, triggering a cascade of stop-loss orders that accelerated the decline. The 50-day moving average is now falling, signalling a weakening short-term trend.

5. May Jobs Report

Bitcoin's descent continued following May's employment report, which showed stronger-than-expected job growth — reducing the probability of a near-term Fed rate cut and sending risk assets lower across the board.

Bitcoin Market Context

Bitcoin holds the distinction of being the original cryptocurrency and remains the most widely followed digital coin in 2026. Its market capitalisation sits around $1.33 trillion, far above runner-up Ethereum, which has a market value of roughly $233 billion.

Over roughly the last decade, the cryptocurrency has had an astonishing rise — over 15,000%, in fact. But its upside also comes with real risk, as cryptocurrencies tend to be wildly unpredictable. Bitcoin has had some sharp dips, occasionally dropping tens of thousands of dollars in just a few months.

What Do Analysts Expect Next?

Technical indicators signal bearish market sentiment on Bitcoin, while the Fear and Greed Index is displaying a score of 12 (Extreme Fear). Over the last 30 days, Bitcoin has had 37% green days and 5.64% price volatility.
